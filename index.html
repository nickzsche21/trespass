How a bot is named

By its User-Agent, against the open ai.robots.txt list of AI agents and a list of search engines, previewers, SEO crawlers and HTTP libraries. A name is a claim, not proof — which is the next part.

How a name is checked

OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, Perplexity, Apple, Common Crawl and DuckDuckGo publish the addresses their crawlers use. A request that claims one of those names from outside its list is an impostor. Anthropic, Meta, ByteDance and most others publish no list, so their requests are marked unverifiable — not innocent, not guilty.

How trespass is judged

By RFC 9309: the group naming the crawler applies, otherwise *; the longest matching rule wins. A named group replaces * — the commonest robots.txt surprise, and it’s flagged. It uses the robots.txt you paste today; rules that changed mid-log can’t be known.
